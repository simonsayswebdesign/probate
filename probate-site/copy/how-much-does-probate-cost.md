# How much does probate cost?

It depends on the size and complexity of the estate and on who does the work. If you apply yourself, the main cost is the court fee. If you use a solicitor, legal fees are normally the largest part of the bill.

Here is how the costs break down.

## 1. The court application fee

Anyone applying for a grant of probate in England and Wales pays an application fee to the court, unless the estate is small. The fee is currently reported to be **£526** for estates over £5,000, after an increase in July 2026. There is no fee if the estate is £5,000 or less. Extra official copies of the grant are charged on top, and are cheaper if you order them when you apply.

This fee is the same whether you apply yourself or a solicitor applies for you. A solicitor will normally pay it first and add it to the bill.

## 2. Solicitor fees

Solicitors charge for probate in three main ways.

### Fixed fee

You agree a price up front for a defined set of work, usually getting the grant, or the grant plus the full estate administration. Fixed fees suit simple estates where the facts are clear: one or two beneficiaries, a valid will, few assets. Read what is excluded. Extras such as selling a house, dealing with inheritance tax or handling a complaint may cost more.

### Hourly rate

You pay for the time spent, at a rate that depends on the firm, the location and who is doing the work. Rates vary a lot between a trainee or paralegal and a partner, and between London and elsewhere. Hourly charging is common for complicated or uncertain estates. Ask for an estimate of total hours and agree a review point, such as when costs reach a set figure.

### Percentage of the estate

Some firms charge a percentage of the estate value, plus VAT. This is easy to understand but can be expensive on larger estates where the work is not much greater. Always check what the percentage covers, and whether the value is the gross estate or only the part that passes through the firm.

## 3. Valuation costs

You will need to know what the estate is worth. For a house, a professional valuation is usually needed for inheritance tax and for the probate application. Estate agents and surveyors charge different amounts, and some give an informal valuation for free if you then instruct them to sell. Valuations of jewellery, art, antiques or shares may be extra.

## 4. Other costs (disbursements)

Disbursements are costs paid to third parties on top of the solicitor's own fees. Typical examples:

- Notices to creditors in the Gazette and a local newspaper, which protect executors from later claims they did not know about
- Bankruptcy searches against beneficiaries
- Land Registry copies
- Fees for tracing lost accounts or pensions
- ID verification

The amounts are usually modest compared with legal fees, but ask for them to be itemised.

## 5. Costs that are not part of "probate" but often come with it

- **Selling a property.** Estate agent and conveyancing fees.
- **Inheritance tax.** Where it applies, it is paid out of the estate and is usually far larger than any fee.
- **Funeral costs.** Normally paid from the estate.
- **Clearing and securing a property,** including insurance for empty properties.
- **Specialist advice,** such as an accountant for the final tax return.

## Ways to keep the cost down

- Get at least two or three written quotes and compare what is included.
- Ask whether they charge fixed fee for the grant and hourly for the rest, or one price for everything.
- Gather the paperwork yourself before you ring.
- Check whether you need a grant at all. See [do I need probate?](/do-i-need-probate/)
- Consider doing part of the work yourself, for example registering the death and gathering the account details, and let the solicitor do the technical parts.

## Can the estate pay the solicitor?

Yes. Reasonable costs of administering the estate are normally paid from the estate itself, not out of the executor's pocket. Keep records and receipts.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does probate cost if I do it myself?

If you apply yourself, the main cost is the court fee (see above) and any copies of the grant. You may also have valuation costs and some disbursements. Doing it yourself saves legal fees but takes time, and mistakes can be costly.

### Who pays for probate?

The cost is normally paid out of the estate. If there is no cash available yet, an executor may need to pay first and claim it back, or ask the bank to release funds to pay the solicitor or the inheritance tax.

### Is there a fixed fee for probate?

Some firms offer one, mostly for straightforward estates. Always check what is included and what would cost extra.

### Are solicitor probate fees regulated?

Solicitors must be open about their pricing, but there is no fixed national scale of fees. Prices differ between firms. <!-- TODO: solicitor to confirm. -->

### Is it cheaper to use a probate company than a solicitor?

It can be, but not always. Check whether the company is regulated, what insurance it has and what happens if something goes wrong. Compare the total cost, not the headline number.

### Do I pay probate fees if the estate is under £5,000?

There is no court application fee for estates of £5,000 or less, and a grant may not be needed at all. <!-- TODO: confirm threshold, see above. -->

### Can a solicitor take their fee from the estate before the beneficiaries are paid?

Yes, properly incurred costs are normally paid from the estate before beneficiaries receive their shares.

### What if I think the solicitor's bill is too high?

Raise it with the firm first, using their complaints procedure. If you are not satisfied, you may be able to go to the Legal Ombudsman, which has time limits (reported as one year from the problem or from when you should have realised, or six months from the firm's final response).
