# Do I need probate?

Sometimes, but not always. Whether you need a grant of probate depends on what the person owned and how they owned it, not on whether they left a will.

## What probate is

"Probate" is the legal right to deal with someone's estate after they die. In practice, people use the word for the court document (the grant of probate) that proves the executor named in a will has authority to act. Where there is no will, or no executor who can act, the court issues a different document called letters of administration. In both cases it is usually called "getting probate".

## When you usually need a grant

You will often need one if the person who died:

- **Owned a property in their sole name,** or as a tenant in common. The Land Registry will normally want to see a grant before it records a sale or transfer.
- **Had money in banks or building societies above the bank's own limit.** Each bank sets its own threshold, and they differ.
- **Held shares or investments** in their own name.
- **Had other assets** that need to be collected or sold, such as a vehicle of high value.

If you are the executor and an organisation says "we need to see the grant", you have your answer for that asset.

## When you may not need one

A grant may not be required if:

- **Everything was owned jointly** and passes automatically to the surviving owner. This is common for married couples and civil partners with a jointly owned home and joint bank account. The survivor usually needs only the death certificate.
- **The estate is small and has no property.** Many banks, building societies and pension providers will release small balances without a grant. Each sets its own limit, so ask each one.
- **Assets pass outside the estate,** for example life insurance written in trust, some pensions with a nominated beneficiary, or assets held in a trust.
- **The person owned very little.** Some estates are only a bank account, a few personal items and a final pension payment.

## Sole or joint?

If you are not sure how property is owned, check the title register at the Land Registry. It costs a small fee. A house owned as joint tenants passes to the survivor automatically. A house owned as tenants in common does not: the deceased's share forms part of their estate. <!-- TODO: confirm Land Registry fee for a copy of the register; the figure is not stated here. -->

## What if there is no will?

You can still apply for a grant if one is needed. It will be letters of administration rather than probate, and the law sets out who can apply and in what order. This is often a spouse or civil partner, then children, and so on. A solicitor can explain who qualifies in your case.

## Do I have to use a solicitor?

No. You can apply for probate yourself through gov.uk. Many people with a simple estate do. The process involves valuing the estate, working out whether inheritance tax is due, and filling in forms accurately. Some people prefer to have a solicitor do some or all of it, particularly if there is a property, no will, inheritance tax, or beneficiaries who disagree.

See [how much probate costs](/how-much-does-probate-cost/) for an idea of what a solicitor charges, or [request a quote](/get-a-quote/).

## What if I am not sure?

Start by listing what the person owned and how it was held. Then contact the organisations involved and ask what they need. If a bank, pension company or the Land Registry asks for a grant, you need one. If you are unsure, a solicitor can give you a short answer in a first call.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need probate if there is a will?

Not necessarily. A will names who inherits and who the executors are, but the executors only need a grant if an organisation holding the assets insists on one.

### Do I need probate for a house?

If the house was in the deceased's sole name, usually yes, before you can sell or transfer it. If it was held jointly with the survivor, usually no.

### Do I need probate for a bank account?

It depends on the balance and the bank. Joint accounts normally pass to the survivor. For a sole account, small balances are often released without a grant, but larger ones need one.

### Do I need probate for a car?

Often not, if the car can be re-registered with the DVLA by the person inheriting it. Check with the DVLA if the value is high or there is a dispute. <!-- TODO: confirm DVLA process. -->

### Do I need probate if the estate is under £5,000?

The court does not charge an application fee for estates of £5,000 or less, but whether you need one still depends on the assets and on what institutions require. <!-- TODO: confirm threshold on gov.uk. -->

### Do I need probate if there is no property?

Not always. Many people deal with small estates with no property and no grant, because the banks and other holders release funds on production of a death certificate. It depends on the amounts.

### Who can apply for probate?

Normally the executors named in the will. If there is no will or no executor, the law sets an order of people who can apply for letters of administration.

### Can I be forced to apply for probate?

If you are named as executor you do not have to act, but you should say so formally if you do not want the role. A solicitor can explain how to renounce or step back. Do not simply ignore the role if you do not want it.

### What happens if I do not get probate when I need it?

You will not be able to sell, transfer or close the relevant assets. The estate cannot be fully administered and beneficiaries cannot be paid.
